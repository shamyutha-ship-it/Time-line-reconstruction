About Forensic Time-Line Reconstruction

Forensic Time-Line Reconstruction is the scientific discipline of synthesizing multi-source digital evidence into a validated chronological ledger. By extracting raw file timestamps, detecting timestomping anomalies, and validating cryptographic hash integrity, investigators can prove exactly when events transpired during an incident or dispute.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is forensic time-line reconstruction?

Forensic time-line reconstruction is the process of correlating disparate digital artifacts—such as file system MACB (Modified, Accessed, Changed, Birth) metadata, capture device EXIF data, and audit records—into an indisputable chronological narrative to understand attack vectors or legal dispute timelines.

How does this online timeline reconstruction tool protect evidence privacy?

All hashing, timestamp extraction, and report formatting execute 100% locally in your web browser via the browser's native Web Cryptography API. No files are uploaded to any cloud server, preserving chain of custody and confidentiality.

What file formats and evidence types can be reconstructed?

The analyzer supports raw digital evidence including JPEG/PNG images (with hardware EXIF timestamp parsing), MP4/MKV video files, PDF contracts, documents, disk image extracts, and forensic CSV timeline exports from tools like Plaso, Log2timeline, and Autopsy.

Can I use this for academic research and formal investigations?

Yes. The platform was built adhering to NIST SP 800-86 digital forensics standards and includes simulated APT intrusion and ransomware scenarios for testing and verification.
